Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics Agency (BMKG), through its Region III Denpasar office, has issued an early warning for tidal flooding at six coastal areas of Bali along with sea waves reaching 4-6 meters in the island's southern waters, effective through Monday, August 17, 2026. The warning stems from a combination of lunar perigee on August 10 and a full moon on August 13, pushing tides to their peak just as Bali heads into its Independence Day long weekend.

## Perigee and Full Moon Drive Peak Tides

This round of tidal flooding differs from the Super New Moon warning that hit Bali's coast in mid-June. This time the trigger is perigee — the moon's closest approach to Earth — which fell on August 10, 2026, followed three days later by a full moon on August 13. The combination of these two astronomical events strengthens the moon's gravitational pull on the sea, driving tides higher than normal throughout the August 11-17 window.

The situation is compounded by an active Australian monsoon, generating strong winds from the east and south at speeds reaching 25 knots, or roughly 46 kilometers per hour. That combination of strong wind and elevated tides is pushing waves in Bali's southern waters and the southern Lombok Strait to a potential 4 to 6 meters — a range BMKG classifies as highly dangerous for small fishing boats and tourist vessels.

## Six Coastal Areas Under Alert

BMKG's Region III Denpasar office has identified six coastal zones in Bali under tidal-flood alert this week:

- The southern coast of Jembrana Regency
- The southern coast of Tabanan Regency
- Badung Regency's coastline, including the tourist-dense areas of Kuta, Seminyak, and Jimbaran
- Denpasar city's coastline
- Gianyar Regency's coastline
- The southern coast of Klungkung Regency

BMKG notes that small-boat fishermen already face elevated risk once wind speeds exceed 15 knots and wave heights reach 1.25 meters — thresholds well surpassed this week. Tourists and marine tourism operators along these coasts are urged to monitor BMKG's official channels and the Info BMKG app before heading out to sea or lingering at the waterline during high tide.

## Impact on the Coastal Economy and Holiday Season

The overlapping tidal flooding and high waves threaten to disrupt several sectors at once. Loading and unloading at small, traditional docks is being hampered by seawater intrusion, while salt farms and inland fisheries along the southern coast risk damage from saltwater seepage. Traditional fishermen in Jembrana and Tabanan are advised to postpone fishing trips over the coming days, since large swells can capsize a small boat within seconds.

The warning arrives right in the middle of a tourist surge ahead of Indonesia's 81st Independence Day long weekend on August 17. Popular beaches in Badung such as Kuta and Seminyak, normally packed with domestic and foreign visitors chasing sunset views, now face temporary erosion and flooding in parking areas and beachside vendor stalls during peak high tide. Villa managers and beachfront restaurants in the area have been told to secure outdoor furniture and equipment out of reach of the incoming tide.

Local governments in the six affected regencies and cities have been asked to keep personnel and heavy equipment on standby for rapid response should flooding spread into densely populated coastal neighborhoods, echoing past tidal-flood events in Denpasar and Badung. Residents in low-lying homes near the shoreline are also advised to prepare simple evacuation routes and move valuables to higher ground while peak tides continue.

BMKG stressed the warning remains dynamic and subject to updates as sea conditions evolve. Officials urged the public to stay alert without panicking, noting that tidal flooding of similar intensity tends to recur with each month's full moon and perigee cycle — underscoring, they said, a growing need for longer-term coastal resilience planning along southern Bali's shoreline.
