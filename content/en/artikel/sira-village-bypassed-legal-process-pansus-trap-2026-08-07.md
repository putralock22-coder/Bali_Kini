PT Bali Turtle Island Development (BTID) went ahead with a soft opening of Sira Village Grand Outlet Bali in the Kura-Kura Bali Special Economic Zone (SEZ) on Serangan Island, Denpasar, on Friday, July 31, 2026 — even though the Bali provincial council's special committee on spatial planning, regional assets, and licensing (Pansus TRAP) had not finished following up on findings of alleged violations in the area. The move drew sharp criticism from lawmakers, who said management had disregarded the legislature and an ongoing legal process.

## Timeline and Council Reaction

Pansus TRAP secretary Dewa Nyoman Rai said the opening of the roughly 4.7-hectare shopping center — a joint project between Mitsubishi Estate Group and PT BTID — took place while nine Pansus TRAP recommendations on KEK Kura-Kura Bali were still awaiting formal follow-up from the Bali provincial government's executive branch. He said BTID management's decision to proceed without waiting for the council's review reflected a disregard for the legislature's oversight function.

"The recommendations we issued are not mere suggestions — they are administrative legal products with binding force," Dewa Nyoman Rai stressed, as quoted by Warta Bali Online.

Ahead of the soft opening, civil society group FOR HATI Bali had already raised concerns about Sira Village's proximity to Pura Sakenan, one of Bali's revered kahyangan jagat temples, arguing the issue needed further review before the commercial zone began full operations.

## Nine Recommendations and Alleged New Activity

Pansus TRAP's nine recommendations on KEK Kura-Kura Bali followed findings of alleged spatial-planning, asset, and licensing violations on Serangan Island — a reclaimed area that has carried unresolved land-status disputes since the late 1990s. Bali Governor Wayan Koster had previously been urged to act swiftly on all nine points, but the executive branch had not issued a formal response by the time the soft opening took place.

Pansus TRAP also flagged suspected new activity at the project site after the council's recommendations were issued, including indications of further reclamation work around the area. If confirmed, the committee said it would conduct a direct field inspection in Serangan to follow up on a number of community reports it has received.

## Impact on Investment and SEZ Governance

The dispute puts KEK Kura-Kura Bali — touted by the government as a premium tourism-based special economic zone and prospective international financial hub — under public scrutiny over the consistency of its licensing oversight. Sira Village had been promoted as Bali's first outlet shopping center, featuring more than 100 global brands, and was expected to boost investment appeal in the Serangan area.

But the standoff between the project's executive team and the legislature risks setting a troubling precedent for other investors considering the zone, particularly if major developers are seen as able to bypass licensing and spatial-planning oversight. For Serangan's indigenous community, the episode also touches a longer-running concern: unresolved land disputes stemming from the island's reclamation, and assurances that sacred sites like Pura Sakenan remain protected from commercial pressure.

Pansus TRAP said it will continue monitoring the case until the provincial government formally responds to its recommendations, while preparing a follow-up inspection of the project site in the near future.
