Dust rose over the wet dirt track of Pengambengan, Jembrana, in late June as the 2026 Makepung competition season officially opened with the Kapolda Bali Cup. For the people of Jembrana, the moment was more than a season opener — it marked the return of a cultural identity that has taken root at the western tip of Bali for nearly a century.

## From Rice-Field Plowing to Racetrack

The word Makepung comes from the Balinese "makepung-kepungan," meaning "to chase one another." The tradition dates back to around the 1920s, when Jembrana farmers plowed their rice paddies communally using water buffalo. During breaks from churning the soil into mud ahead of planting season, farmers began racing their buffalo across the wet fields for fun — a casual pastime that gradually evolved into a formal contest of skill, complete with rules and dedicated circuits.

Jembrana is known as an agrarian region with a buffalo population raised by locals for generations, originally to plow rice fields before the planting season. Just as Madura has its bull races, Bali has Makepung — and its particular version of the tradition exists only in Jembrana, found nowhere else on the island.

## Race Format and the Kapolda Bali Cup

Unlike Madura's bull races, which run in a straight sprint over a short track, Makepung pits pairs of buffalo pulling a two-wheeled wooden cart, called a cikar, around a U-shaped or semicircular circuit roughly two kilometers long. Jockeys stand atop the cart, steering their buffalo with a whip and reins as they race against the clock and against the pair running behind them.

The 2026 season officially opened with the Kapolda Bali Cup at the Pengambengan circuit on June 28, followed by a series of regular championship rounds held every few weeks on Sundays across circuits in different sub-districts — including Loloan Timur (Bupati Cup, August 26), Awen and Lelateng (September 9), Delodberawah (September 23), and Mertasari (October 7). The season peaks with the Bali Governor's Cup in October, long regarded as the most prestigious race and the biggest crowd-draw of the year. The competition calendar closes with the Jembrana Cup in November.

## A Heritage Still Carefully Kept

Each sekaa, or team, typically cares for its buffalo with particular attention — from diet and regular bathing to decorative horn coverings made of colorful cloth and small bells that ring loudly as the animals gallop. For Jembrana residents, owning a top Makepung buffalo is a matter of pride that goes beyond the racetrack; it is part of a family and banjar (neighborhood) identity passed down across generations.

All Makepung races are free and open to the public, and organizers typically advise spectators to arrive at the circuit before 7:30 a.m., since races start early while the soil is still wet and ideal footing for the buffalo. Beyond its cultural draw, Makepung also supports the local economy through food and souvenir vendors around the circuits, and it is increasingly catching the attention of travelers looking for a side of Bali beyond Kuta and Ubud — the island's agrarian heart, rarely in the spotlight but very much alive in Jembrana.
