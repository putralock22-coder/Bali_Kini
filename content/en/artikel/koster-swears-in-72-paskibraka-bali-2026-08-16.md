Bali Governor Wayan Koster officially swore in 72 members of Bali Province's 2026 Paskibraka flag-bearer unit on Friday, August 15, in Denpasar. The top students, drawn from all nine of Bali's regencies and cities, will raise the Red-and-White flag at the Detik-Detik Proklamasi ceremony marking the 81st anniversary of Indonesian Independence at Lapangan Niti Mandala Renon on August 17, 2026. At the swearing-in, Koster also pledged that the provincial government would identify Paskibraka members from lower-income families and provide them with social assistance and continued education support.

## A Rigorous Selection From Nine Regencies

The 72 members of Bali's 2026 Paskibraka were chosen from an initial pool of 90 candidates who underwent training beforehand. Sixteen candidates were returned to their home regencies or cities to serve at the local level, while two selected students go on to represent Bali at the national ceremony in Jakarta — one of them a student from Karangasem who grew up an orphan. Regional security and defense officials, including the Korem 163/Wira Satya military command, attended the swearing-in and supported the physical and mental training the candidates underwent during their quarantine period.

Paskibraka members are chosen based on academic achievement, physical fitness, and assessments of character and leadership, making the title one of the highest honors a Bali high school student can earn. Every year, tiered selection running from the regency level up to the province sifts through thousands of applicants before narrowing the field to the dozens of top students who complete the intensive training ahead of Independence Day.

## Koster: Achievement Should Not Be Held Back by Economics

Responding to the presence of Paskibraka members from economically disadvantaged families, Koster said the provincial government would step in. "We'll check the data — in principle it's fine, we'll help," Koster said, referring to possible social assistance or continued scholarships for the high-achieving students. The statement reinforces the Bali provincial government's stated direction: ensuring that student achievement is never cut short by a lack of funds for education.

The commitment follows a pattern already established by local governments across Bali, where the Paskibraka title has often opened doors to scholarships through university for high-achieving students from low-income families. For many families in Bali's outlying areas, a child's selection to the provincial or national Paskibraka unit is a source of pride in itself — and a rare pathway to educational mobility that might otherwise be out of reach.

## Independence Day Ceremony at Renon

Koster is confirmed to personally lead the Detik-Detik Proklamasi ceremony marking the 81st anniversary of Indonesian Independence at Lapangan Niti Mandala Renon in Denpasar, a monument that also stands as a symbol of the Balinese people's struggle. The ceremony will follow the official guidelines issued by Indonesia's Ministry of State Secretariat, including the raising of the heirloom flag by Paskibraka members, a reading of the Proclamation text, and readings of Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution's preamble.

The swearing-in of the 72 Paskibraka members caps a long preparation process that began with selection at the regency and city level months earlier, followed by a centralized joint training camp at the provincial level during quarantine. Beyond drilling in marching and ceremonial procedure, the students were also given lessons in national values and the spirit of struggle, so they understand the deeper meaning behind the symbolic duty they carry out.

For Bali, the moment coincides with the 68th anniversary of Bali as a province, observed in mid-August, layering extra significance onto this year's Independence Day events for local residents — celebrating both the nation's independence and Bali's own long journey as an autonomous province. Koster said he hoped the spirit of service shown by the Paskibraka members would spread more broadly among Bali's younger generation, well beyond the ceremonial moment of August 17 itself.
