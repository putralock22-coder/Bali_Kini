Desa Adat Batur in Kintamani, Bangli Regency, is holding a week-long Batur Village Festival from August 2-8, 2026, to mark a century since Rarud Batur — the mass exodus forced by Mount Batur's devastating 1926 eruption, which buried the community's original settlement under lava. The festival carries the theme "Batur Matangi, Sebuah Kesadaran akan Kebangkitan" (Batur Awakens, an Awareness of Resurrection), underscoring a century of collective resilience since the disaster.

## A History Buried in Lava

The major eruption of Mount Batur, which unfolded from August through September 1926, completely reshaped the landscape at the volcano's foot. Lava flows buried the settlement of Desa Batur Let along with Pura Ulun Danu Batur, then the center of the community's religious and social life. The disaster forced villagers to evacuate and rebuild their lives and temple in a safer location on the northeastern side of the caldera — the origin of today's Desa Batur and the Pura Ulun Danu Batur complex on the shore of Lake Batur.

Rarud, in Balinese, means mass exodus or evacuation. For the Batur community, the centennial of Rarud Batur is more than a record of natural disaster — it is a milestone that shapes how they understand resilience and renewal after profound loss. Ahead of the commemoration, the traditional village even erected a historical marker at the exact site of the original settlement, so future generations can trace their ancestors' footsteps.

## A Week of Commemorative Events

The festival opened with the Batur Caldera Run, taking participants along the volcano's caldera, followed by a centennial seminar on the relocation featuring historians and traditional leaders, the festival's official opening, and a theatrical performance of Rarud Batur retelling the story of the community's displacement after the 1926 eruption. On August 3, 2026, a heritage trail walk retraced the route from the old settlement to the new village — following in the footsteps their ancestors took a century ago.

The program also includes a Kintamani coffee seminar highlighting the economic potential of the highland's arabica coffee, a cultural parade, a cultural literacy competition for students, a traditional dress showcase, a traditional gamelan competition, folk games, and a closing reception. Organizers said the entire program was designed not merely as ceremony, but as a collective movement to revive the history, culture, and spirit of their ancestors among the village's younger generation.

## Impact on Tourism and Cultural Preservation in Kintamani

The festival has the potential to boost visitor numbers to the Kintamani and Lake Batur area, a destination long associated with caldera scenery but with relatively little emphasis on its history and local culture. By bringing the story of the 1926 eruption to life through the heritage trail and theatrical performance, Desa Adat Batur is offering a form of cultural and educational tourism distinct from the nature-focused packages that have long dominated the area.

For the people of Batur, the festival is also a chance to pass on historical knowledge to a younger generation who did not live through the Rarud, while reinforcing the traditional village's identity amid Bali's fast-growing mass tourism. Bangli Regency's government and local tourism operators hope the Rarud Batur centennial can become an annual cultural fixture that enriches Bali's event calendar beyond established hubs like Ubud or Kuta.
